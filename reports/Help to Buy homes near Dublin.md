# Balbriggan wins value, Clonburris wins the clock

For a Help to Buy (HTB) first-time buyer who needs a new 3-bed house within roughly an hour of Dublin city centre and keys by the end of 2027, the November 2026 market is brutally thin: inside a realistic 60-minute door-to-door commute only four open-market schemes still sell a 3-bed *house* at or under the €500,000 HTB cap (Keepers Lock in Clonburris at about €480,000, Holybanks in Swords from €495,000, and Balmoston in Donabate and Regles in Lusk at exactly €500,000), and the genuine value sits 10–20 minutes beyond the hour in Balbriggan, where Glenveagh's Folkstown Park sells A-rated 3-beds at **€465,000–€485,000** with both HTB and First Home Scheme (FHS) eligibility and completions running through 2026, and the Fingal/LDA Hampton Demesne starter-home portals (late 2026 and mid-2027) offer equivalent 98–108 m² houses at **€370,000–€475,000** against a €470,000–€500,000 market value. Our recommendation is therefore conditional on one question you must answer: if 75 minutes door-to-door is tolerable, buy **Folkstown Park, Balbriggan** (or apply for Hampton Demesne if your household earns under about €107,000); if the hour is a hard line, buy **Keepers Lock, Clonburris** at roughly €480,000, where Clondalkin Fonthill is 28 minutes from Connolly. The runners-up are the Kildare County Council/LDA **Barracksfield West** starter homes in Naas (3-bed from €365,216, next tranche launching December 2026, about 75 minutes door-to-door) and **Balmoston, Donabate** (€500,000, the fastest rail commute of any option but zero price headroom and weak local schooling). Everything genuinely cheap, from Drogheda (€380,000–€395,000) and Athy (€390,000–€415,000) to Gorey, Portlaoise, Tullamore, Mullingar and Carlow (€370,000–€415,000), is 80–150 minutes door-to-door and fails your constraint. On financing, Budget 2027 lifted HTB to **€35,000 for contracts signed on or after 7 October 2026**, and stacked with the maximum 20% FHS equity the household income you need is about **17.5% of the price: €81,400 at €465,000, €84,000 at €480,000 and €87,500 at €500,000**, with HTB covering all but €11,500–€15,000 of the 10% deposit.

## The hour line leaves four houses and three council routes

Your one-hour constraint is the single most selective filter in this search, and the evidence says most of the "commuter belt" fails it once you add a realistic 10–15 minutes at each end of the train. On the Northern line the 06:05 Drogheda service reaches Connolly at 07:09, calling Balbriggan at 06:19, Skerries 06:25, Rush & Lusk 06:32 and Donabate 06:38, which puts Donabate at 31 minutes, Rush & Lusk at 37, Skerries at 44 and Balbriggan at 50 minutes station-to-station ([Irish Rail](https://www.irishrail.ie/en-ie/news/new-0605hrs-drogheda-to-dublin-connolly-morning-service-from-monday-20th-april)). Drogheda itself is 60–64 minutes on the timetable ([bustimes.org](https://bustimes.org/services/rail-dublin-droghedadundalk)), but commuters from Drogheda and Dundalk told RTÉ in March 2026 that they spend "1.5 to 2 hours door-to-door each way", with Drogheda's roughly 350 station car-park spaces full by 7am and clamping rising from 6 cars in 2023 to 98 in 2025 ([RTÉ, 25 Mar 2026](https://rte.ie/news/leinster/2026/0325/1565053-commuting-north-east/); [Drogheda Life](https://droghedalife.com/?p=123450)). On the Kildare line, Adamstown is about 34 minutes and Clondalkin Fonthill about 28 minutes from Connolly on a sample Phoenix Park Tunnel service ([signal.eu.org](https://signal.eu.org/rail/train/ir/4298_4292)), Sallins–Heuston is quoted at 33–50 minutes ([Rome2rio](https://www.rome2rio.com/s/Naas/Dublin)), and Athy is "about 50 minutes" to Heuston on almost-hourly trains ([Irish Times, 25 Sept 2025](https://www.irishtimes.com/property/residential/2025/09/25/three-and-four-bed-homes-from-390000-qualify-for-first-time-buyer-schemes-in-athy-co-kildare)). On the Maynooth line, direct trains take 30–50 minutes ([Irish Times, 27 Aug 2026](https://www.irishtimes.com/property/residential/2026/08/27/premium-three-and-four-bed-homes-at-cairns-new-maynooth-development-from-630000/)), and M3 Parkway/Dunboyne has direct peak trains to Docklands in roughly 39 minutes but only a Clonsilla shuttle off-peak ([Wikipedia](https://EN.wikipedia.com/wiki/M3_Parkway_railway_station); [Rome2rio](https://www.rome2rio.com/s/MSD-Dunboyne/Dublin)). South of the city the official Rosslare-line and DART timetables give Bray 48–51 minutes to Connolly, Greystones 53–62, Kilcoole 67, Wicklow 77–78, Arklow 110 and Gorey 123–125 minutes, with only two usable peak-arrival trains a day from Wicklow southwards ([Irish Rail Rosslare timetable](https://www.irishrail.ie/_kontent/d2e85689-fbf9-010a-a02e-587ed94b5032/4299f551-bb35-4e25-a695-af348f2ce0d4/2eaa16f8-4645-4cd7-a1a2-83c1665f701b.pdf); [Irish Rail DART Table 18](https://www.irishrail.ie/_kontent/d2e85689-fbf9-010a-a02e-587ed94b5032/f354b22d-018d-4216-b6dc-4b6a4f774fff/Table%2018%20DART%20valid%20to%2012.12.2026.pdf)). Navan has no rail; the NX bus went to a 15-minute peak frequency and a new Moss Street terminus on 11 October 2026 but publishes no journey time, and third-party figures range from 54 to 88 minutes ([TFI](https://www.transportforireland.ie/news/new-nx-timetable-to-deliver-more-peak-time-services-between-navan-and-dublin-from-11-october/); [Transit](https://transitapp.com/en/region/dublin/bus-%C3%A9ireann/bus-nx)). The midlands are hopeless on this test: Tullamore and Carlow are roughly 55–65 minutes by rail, Mullingar 74–81 minutes, before any access time ([Rome2rio Tullamore](https://www.rome2rio.com/Train/Tullamore/Heuston); [bustimes.org Dublin–Sligo](https://bustimes.org/services/rail-dublin-sligo)).

None of the rail projects that would change this picture will be running by the end of 2027. Irish Rail's December 2026 timetable consultation states that peak frequency "cannot currently be increased, as all available fleet and infrastructure capacity is in use" ([Irish Rail](https://www.irishrail.ie/ga-ie/news/timetable-consultation-2026)); DART+ West has its Railway Order and CPOs under way but no contractor or opening date, with sources placing construction anywhere from a 2027 start to 2028–2032 ([dartplus.ie](https://www.dartplus.ie/en-ie/projects/dart-west); [Irish Times, 7 Sept 2026](https://irishtimes.com/transport/2026/09/07/more-than-200-property-owners-face-compulsory-purchase-orders-for-dart-west)); DART+ Coastal North to Drogheda is approved but completion dates conflict between 2029 and 2035 ([TFI](https://www.transportforireland.ie/news/an-coimisiun-pleanala-approves-dart-coastal-north-railway-order/); [IrishCycle, 1 Jul 2026](https://irishcycle.com/2026/07/01/work-on-dart-coastal-north-to-be-accelerated-using-shared-island-funding/)); and the Navan rail line will not open for about a decade, with a Railway Order planned for 2028 and construction from 2030 ([RTÉ, 24 May 2026](https://www.rte.ie/news/2026/0524/1574920-navan-railine/)). What *will* change is the fare: the NTA has determined an average 15% rise in adult PSO fares from 2027, the first broad increase since 2018 ([NTA](https://www.nationaltransport.ie/news/changes-announced-to-public-transport-fares-from-2027-while-free-travel-and-key-discounts-maintained/)). ## Budget 2027 fixes the stack at €35,000 HTB, 20% equity and a 17.5% income rule

The rules you will actually buy under are now settled for 2027, with one timing trap. Budget 2027, delivered on 6 October 2026, raised the maximum HTB refund from €30,000 to €35,000 and deliberately left the €500,000 price ceiling untouched, the Minister arguing the cap "helps control the overall costs of new builds" ([RTÉ, 6 Oct 2026](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2026/1006/1594234-housing-budget-2027/); [Irish Times, 7 Oct 2026](https://www.irishtimes.com/your-money/2026/10/07/minister-rules-out-raising-cap-on-help-to-buy-scheme-for-homes-worth-over-500k/)). The higher cap applies only if you "sign the contract for your new house between 7 October 2026 and 31 December 2029", so the **contract date, not the completion date, fixes your cap**; a contract signed before 7 October 2026 gets €30,000 even if the house completes in 2027 ([Citizens Information](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/help-to-buy-scheme/); [Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/property/help-to-buy-incentive/how-much-can-you-claim.aspx)). The refund is the lesser of €35,000, 10% of the price, or the income tax and DIRT you paid in the four years before the application year (USC and PRSI do not count), which means the full €35,000 binds only at prices of €350,000 or more and only if you have paid that much tax ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/property/help-to-buy-incentive/how-much-can-you-claim.aspx)). Two mechanics matter for a 2027 completion: an HTB application expires on 31 December of the year it is made unless a claim is lodged (Revenue's manual gives the example of an applicant who applied in August 2021, signed in January 2022 and had to re-apply), so apply in the calendar year you expect to sign, and the mortgage must be at least 70% of the price from a qualifying lender, with FHS equity *excluded* from that 70% test but local-authority starter-home equity *included* for contracts since October 2023 ([Revenue TDM Part 15-01-46](https://www.revenue.ie/en/tax-professionals/tdm-wm/income-tax-capital-gains-tax-corporation-tax/part-15/15-01-46.pdf); [Citizens Information](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/help-to-buy-scheme/)). You must be fully tax compliant for four years, live in the house for five (clawback 100% in year one tapering to 20% in year five), and there is no "fresh start" exception for HTB ([Citizens Information](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/help-to-buy-scheme/)).

The First Home Scheme is where county choice bites. FHS funds up to 30% of the price as shared equity, or **20% if you also use HTB**, charges nothing for five years and then 1.75% a year from year six, and has no income limit but requires you to borrow the maximum a participating lender (AIB/EBS/Haven, Bank of Ireland, PTSB) will give you under the 4-times-income rule and to put down 10%, which HTB can fund ([FHS FAQs](https://www.firsthomescheme.ie/faqs/); [Citizens Information FHS](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/first-home-scheme/)). Its ceilings are **€500,000 in the four Dublin councils and Wicklow, €475,000 in Kildare and Meath, €425,000 in Louth, €400,000 in Laois, Westmeath and Wexford, and €375,000 in Carlow and Offaly**, last changed on 1 January 2026 and reviewed twice yearly, so a January 2027 change is possible but unannounced ([firsthomescheme.ie](https://www.firsthomescheme.ie/property-price-ceilings/); [MyHome](https://news.myhome.ie/myhome-living/first-home-scheme-extended-in-17-areas-nationwide-37053)). The Local Authority Home Loan is a different product: since 1 April 2026 it lends up to 90% to single applicants earning up to €80,000 or joint applicants up to €85,000, within house-price caps of €415,000 in Dublin, Kildare and Wicklow, €375,000 in Meath, €345,000 in Louth, Westmeath and Wexford and €310,000 in Laois, Offaly and Carlow, at 4.00% fixed for up to 25 years or 4.05% for 26–30 years, and it cannot be combined with FHS ([Citizens Information LAHL](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/local-authority-mortgages/); [localauthorityhomeloan.ie](https://localauthorityhomeloan.ie/)). Because the best commercial fixed rates were around 3.0–3.5% in September 2026 and the Central Bank's June 2026 average on new mortgages was 3.49%, the LAHL is now a safety net for borrowers refused by two banks rather than a cheaper rate ([mortgagebible.ie](https://mortgagebible.ie/mortgage-rates-ireland-2026/); [Irish Times, 12 Aug 2026](https://www.irishtimes.com/your-money/2026/08/12/mortgage-interest-rates-up-slightly-but-dip-below-euro-zone-average/)). The renamed Starter Home Purchase Scheme (the old Local Authority Affordable Purchase Scheme) sells council- or LDA-partnered homes at a discount matched by a council equity stake of 5–40%, repayable after 40 years or on sale; you qualify only if four times your gross income is below 85.5% of the open-market value, you can stack HTB on it, but you cannot use FHS ([Citizens Information Starter Home Purchase Scheme](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/local-authority-affordable-purchase-scheme/); [FHS FAQs](https://www.firsthomescheme.ie/faqs/)). Central Bank limits are unchanged since January 2023 at 4 times income and 90% loan-to-value for first-time buyers, with a bridging-loan tweak in April 2026 the only amendment; a regional-press claim that the multiple rose to 5x is wrong ([Central Bank](https://www.centralbank.ie/consumer-hub/explainers/what-are-the-mortgage-measures); [Central Bank, 8 Apr 2026](https://www.centralbank.ie/news/article/press-release-targeted-amendment-to-mortgage-measures-home-bridging-loans-8-april-2026)).

Put together, the arithmetic is simple. With HTB and the maximum 20% FHS stake, your mortgage must be exactly 70% of the price (which also satisfies HTB's 70% test), so the income you need is 70% ÷ 4 = **17.5% of the price**; without FHS it is 90% ÷ 4 = **22.5%**. The scheme-rules research tabulated this as €63,875 (with FHS) or €82,125 (without) at €365,000, €70,000 or €90,000 at €400,000, €78,750 or €101,250 at €450,000, and €87,500 or €112,500 at €500,000, with monthly repayments over 30 years at 3.5% of roughly €1,147–€2,021 and the savings needed on top of HTB rising from €1,500 at €365,000 to €15,000 at €500,000 ([Citizens Information FHS](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/first-home-scheme/); [Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/property/help-to-buy-incentive/how-much-can-you-claim.aspx)). Note the perverse incentive: because FHS forces you to borrow the maximum, every extra €1 of income shrinks the FHS stake by €4, and a household earning more than 22.5% of the price gets no FHS at all.

## County by county, the sub-€450,000 house sits 20–60 minutes beyond the hour

The regional inventories reveal a market that has bunched the "entry" 3-bed house at or just under €500,000 wherever land is within an hour of the city, and pushed anything cheaper out along the motorways. A working paper using 768,074 Property Price Register transactions finds new builds bunch just below €500,000 at 2.33 times the expected density (2.62 in Dublin), with roughly €11,700 of price compression per bunched sale and no bunching at all in second-hand homes ([APEP, Apr 2026](https://ape.socialcatalystlab.org/papers/apep_1297/v1/paper.pdf)); you can see it on the ground in Balmoston, Corballis View, Regles, Hamlet Wood and Millers Glen all pricing their 3-bed at exactly €500,000 ([Glenveagh](https://glenveagh.ie/developments); [Dublish](https://dublish.ie/new-homes/)). Daft's Q3 2026 data put the median new-build price at €500,000 in north Dublin and €650,000 in south Dublin against a national new-build median of €448,000 ([Irish Times, 7 Oct 2026](https://www.irishtimes.com/your-money/2026/10/07/minister-rules-out-raising-cap-on-help-to-buy-scheme-for-homes-worth-over-500k/)).

On the **County Dublin fringe**, the only open-market 3-bed houses verifiably at or under the cap in November 2026 are Folkstown Park in Balbriggan (The Pine 3-bed end-terrace €465,000, The Beech semi €485,000, Phase 2 under construction with first completions "during 2026"), Hamlet Wood in Balbriggan (The Oak €500,000, listed May 2026 and stated to qualify for HTB and FHS, though some panels show €520,000–€530,000), Balmoston in Donabate (The Poplar end-terrace €500,000, Phase 2 completions from Q1 2026), Corballis View in Donabate (3-bed €500,000, "coming soon"), Regles in Lusk (The Shenick semi from €500,000), Holybanks in Swords (€495,000 entry) and Keepers Lock in Clonburris (3-bed mid-terrace from €480,000 in March 2026, with a 53 Keepers Lock Park sale registered at €480,176 in July 2026 but an end-terrace listed at €520,000) ([Glenveagh Balbriggan](https://glenveagh.ie/our-communities/balbriggan); [Daft Hamlet Wood](https://www.daft.ie/new-home-for-sale/hamlet-wood-hamlet-lane-balbriggan-co-dublin/6565736); [Daft Balmoston](https://www.daft.ie/new-home-for-sale/the-poplar-balmoston-donabate-co-dublin/5610595); [Irish Times, 24 Sept 2026](https://www.irishtimes.com/property/residential/2026/09/24/new-homes-your-guide-to-developments-on-the-market-across-ireland/); [Dublish Keepers Lock](https://dublish.ie/new-homes/keepers-lock); [MyHome price register](https://www.myhome.ie/priceregister/53-keepers-lock-park-keepers-lock-clonburris-dublin-388817)). Everything in Adamstown, Lucan, Citywest, Newcastle, Hansfield, Kilternan, Cherrywood and Kinsealy now starts at €520,000–€710,000 for a 3-bed house, and Glenveagh's Swords houses sold out during 2026 ([Irish Times, 24 Sept 2026](https://www.irishtimes.com/property/residential/2026/09/24/new-homes-your-guide-to-developments-on-the-market-across-ireland/); [Glenveagh](https://glenveagh.ie/developments)). The council route is much richer here than anywhere else: Fingal lists Hampton Demesne (3-bed mid-terrace 97.8 m², market value €470,000, minimum €370,000, maximum €446,500, income limit €100,463; 3-bed end-terrace 108 m², market value €500,000, minimum €400,000, maximum €475,000, income limit €106,875; further portals "late 2026 and mid 2027"), Golding Green in Kinsealy (3-bed The Beech €440,000), Balmoston Phase 2, Folkstown Park and Church Fields "additional units" as current starter-home schemes, and completed its 1,000th affordable sale in June 2026 ([Fingal Hampton Demesne](https://www.fingal.ie/ga/node/8092); [Fingal Starter Homes](https://www.fingal.ie/starterhomes); [costrentalradar](https://costrentalradar.ie/guides/affordable-homes-dublin/)). ## Schools, childcare and GPs separate the finalists more than price does

Once the hour filter and the price cap have done their work, what remains is a handful of Fingal, D22, Dunboyne and Naas options, and here the schools-and-amenities research changes the order. **Balbriggan** has four post-primaries including Bremore Educate Together Secondary, a Balbriggan Educate Together primary, and a new DDLETB community special school that opened admissions in May 2026, plus the most heavily funded amenity pipeline in the study: the €15m Quay Street and harbour scheme, a €3.3m creative hub due end-2027, playgrounds completed at Millpond, Bremore and Charlie Moore parks, and an €11m 25-metre public pool at Castlelands with planning approved in May 2026 and construction due to start in late 2026 for 2028 ([Bremore ETSS](https://www.bremoreetss.ie/Balbriggan/Index.html); [DDLETB](https://www.ddletb.ie/new-community-special-schools-admissions-notice/); [Dublin People, 15 Jan 2026](https://dublinpeople.com/news/northsideeast/articles/2026/01/15/2026-will-be-another-year-of-massive-progress-for-our-balbriggan/); [Dublin People, 20 May 2026](https://dublinpeople.com/news/northsideeast/articles/2026/05/20/planning-approval-clears-way-for-balbriggan-swimming-pool/)). Its weaknesses are childcare and the unknown: Balbriggan has a single community crèche in a county with only 16 public versus 274 private services and 1,038 children on waiting lists, Fingal's median full-day fee of about €231 a week is the third highest of 30 divisions, and no 2025 Leaving Cert progression rate for any Fingal school could be retrieved ([Dublin Inquirer](https://www.dublininquirer.com/in-fingal-community-creches-are-few-and-far-between-and-falling/); [Pobal](https://www.pobal.ie/data-library/early-learning-and-childcare-data/)). **Donabate**, by contrast, has one post-primary, 339 peninsula pupils travelling out to secondary school in 2023/24, developers asking to scrap planned crèches, and a council that approved more homes in 2024 "despite concerns about lack of accompanying services" ([Dublin Inquirer, 4 Sep 2024](https://dublininquirer.com/2024/09/04/in-donabate-council-okays-more-homes-despite-concerns-about-lack-of-accompanying-services-and-amenities/)). **Swords** is the strongest amenity town in the entire north-east, with six post-primaries including Thornleigh ETSS, the Pavilions centre with an 11-screen cinema, the 220-acre Ward River Valley Park and a €40m library and 165-seat theatre on schedule to open in 2027, but no public pool and the same Fingal childcare problem ([Fingal Swords Cultural Quarter](https://www.fingal.ie/swordsculturalquarter); [Dublin Inquirer, 16 Apr 2025](https://dublininquirer.com/2025/04/16/councillors-push-for-public-swimming-pool-and-sports-hub-for-swords/)).

**Clonburris/Lucan** is a new-town bet. Kishoge Community College and Lucan East Educate Together already operate inside the SDZ and six to eight further school sites are ring-fenced, but none is confirmed to open by 2027; Adamstown Community College had 537 applications for 180 first-year places, a councillor's guide calls Lucan primary places "oversubscribed at present", and the local TD said on 6 November 2026 that Lucan, Clondalkin and Citywest need more "facilities and shared spaces" ([Seven Mills](https://sevenmills.com/community-overview/); [Adamstown CC admission notice](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/65d320da2851005fde29458d/t/68d66a30f16b8e59d478e8a6/1758882352420/_Admission+Notice+1st+yr+2026-27.docx.pdf); [Paul Gogarty](https://www.paulgogarty.com/post/lucan-schools-info); [Dublin People, 6 Nov 2026](https://dublinpeople.com/news/southside/articles/2026/11/06/moynihan-dub-mid-west/)). On the positive side the Department's 2026–27 tranche funds a new Adamstown primary building, a Lucan Boys modular extension and a Lucan Community College extension, and Dublin-wide childcare, though expensive, is at least plentiful in supply terms compared with the 13,500 under-3s waiting across the city ([gov.ie NDP tranche](https://assets.gov.ie/static/documents/5d588bf1/105_Prioritised_Projects_2026-2027.pdf); [Dublin People, 3 Sep 2025](https://dublinpeople.com/news/northsideeast/articles/2025/09/03/fingals-childcare-crisis-revealed/)).

**Naas** ranks second of 30 south-western towns on the schools evidence: St Mary's College posted a 93% progression rate in the 2025 Irish Times feeder data, with CBS Naas, Piper's Hill and Naas Community College all at 76–77%, a new 1,000-pupil St Mary's building got its letter of intent in March 2026, K Leisure's pool holds a PoolMark award and Naas General has a 24-hour ED ([Kfm, 15 Jan 2026](https://www.kfmradio.com/news/localnews/kildare-schools-record-strong-progression-to-third-level-according-to-feeder-schools-data/); [Leinster Leader](https://www.leinsterleader.ie/news/letter-of-intent-confirmed-for-major-school-project-in-kildare-6608520)). But eight Naas primaries reported junior-infant places full for 2026–27 and about 30 children lacked a confirmed September place in February 2026 before extra classes were found, Naas General had 20 patients on trolleys on 6 November 2026, Kildare has 3,423 children on early-years waiting lists (up more than 1,000 in a year) with full-time care averaging €937 a month and some providers at €1,450, and a Newbridge councillor reported trying for six years to register with a GP ([Kildare Nationalist](https://www.kildare-nationalist.ie/news/all-junior-infant-places-filled-in-naas-schools_arid-85466.html); [Meath Chronicle, 3 Apr 2026](https://www.meathchronicle.ie/2026/04/03/cllr-makes-a-splash-about-the-need-for-a-swimming-pool-in-ratoath/); [Irish Times, 25 Jul 2026](https://www.irishtimes.com/health/2026/07/25/gp-shortages-where-are-the-most-acute-problems-across-the-country/)). **Dunshaughlin**, despite its 86/100 "family" score on relocation sites and a €437,500 3-bed duplex at The Willows, should be struck off: all three town primaries were oversubscribed for September 2026 with around 80 children on waiting lists in February, parents in The Willows and Cnoc Tiarnach were among those affected, Dunshaughlin CNS is in prefabs at the GAA grounds, and the permanent campus for it and the 1,000-pupil Coláiste Ríoga had no builder on site five years after planning ([Meath Chronicle, 26 Feb 2026](https://www.meathchronicle.ie/2026/02/26/school-place-stress-they-know-the-demand-here-yet-it-still-happens-and-is-getting-worse/); [Meath Live, 23 Nov 2025](https://meathlive.net/2025/11/23/change-in-minister-must-not-further-delay-dunshaughlin-school-buildings/)).

### Worked financing numbers for the finalists

The table assumes a contract signed on or after 7 October 2026 (€35,000 HTB), at least €35,000 of income tax and DIRT paid over the four look-back years, a 30-year term, and the 3.5% rate that the Central Bank's mid-2026 averages and September 2026 broker tables support; monthly figures are annuity calculations at about €4.49 per €1,000 borrowed, not lender quotes, and exclude stamp duty, legal fees and mortgage protection ([Irish Times, 12 Aug 2026](https://www.irishtimes.com/your-money/2026/08/12/mortgage-interest-rates-up-slightly-but-dip-below-euro-zone-average/); [mortgagebible.ie](https://mortgagebible.ie/mortgage-rates-ireland-2026/)). "Route A" is HTB plus a 90% bank mortgage; "Route B" is HTB plus the maximum 20% FHS stake plus a 70% mortgage, which is the minimum-income route and the one most first-time buyers in this price band will use.

Three things fall out of these numbers. First, Route B puts Folkstown Park (€81,400 income), Barracksfield (€82,200) and Keepers Lock (€84,000) within a few thousand euro of each other on the income you need, so the choice between them is really about location, equity partner (FHS versus council) and house size rather than affordability; the council schemes win on monthly cost only because their equity is a bigger share of a cheaper headline price. Second, HTB covers the whole deposit only up to €350,000, so every open-market finalist needs €11,500–€15,000 of savings on top of HTB, while the starter-home routes need €1,500–€5,000 ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/property/help-to-buy-incentive/how-much-can-you-claim.aspx)). Third, the starter-home discount is income-tested in a way that screens out higher earners: at Barracksfield the price you pay is set by your mortgage capacity, so a household on €100,000 pays close to €440,000 rather than €365,216, and above €102,600 you are ineligible, while at Hampton Demesne the same logic runs from €400,000 up to €475,000 ([KCC Barracksfield Phase 2A](https://kildarecoco.ie/AllServices/Housing/AffordableHousing/BarracksfieldWestPhase2Naas/); [Citizens Information Starter Home Purchase Scheme](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/local-authority-affordable-purchase-scheme/)). Practically, that means a two-earner household on €110,000-plus should ignore the council routes and compare Folkstown Park with Keepers Lock on Route A, where the Balbriggan house is €1,879 a month against €1,940 in Clonburris.

### Where the sources conflict or have gone stale

Several points in the underlying research disagree, and you should treat the following as unresolved until checked. The regional listing notes repeatedly quote HTB at €30,000 because they pre-date or ignore Budget 2027; Revenue and Citizens Information are explicit that the €35,000 cap applies to contracts from 7 October 2026, and a William Fry summary giving "7 October 2027" is contradicted by both official sources ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/property/help-to-buy-incentive/how-much-can-you-claim.aspx); [William Fry](https://www.williamfry.com/knowledge/budget-2027/)). Kfm reported Barracksfield's €365,000 3-bed as "€5,000 over" the Local Authority Home Loan cap, but that refers to the pre-April 2026 Kildare limit of €360,000, since raised to €415,000, so LAHL is now usable there ([Kfm](https://www.kfmradio.com/news/national/new-affordable-naas-homes-cost-more-than-families-are-allowed-to-borrow-from-council-again); [Citizens Information LAHL](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/local-authority-mortgages/)). Keepers Lock's €480,000 (Dublish, March 2026) versus €520,000 (Daft end-terrace), Hamlet Wood's €500,000 versus €520,000–€530,000 panels, Balmoston's €500,000 versus one €520,000 panel, Gort Mell's €410,000–€425,000 versus a property.ie "from €495,000", Drayton Dundalk's €425,000 versus a reported €375,000, and the Kells schemes (Westfield Drive, Steeple View) that no longer appear on Daft's index are all cases where the lower price may be stale ([Daft Keepers Lock](https://www.daft.ie/new-home-for-sale/keepers-lock-clonburris-dublin-22/6518037); [property.ie Drogheda](https://www.property.ie/new-homes/louth/drogheda/)). Price drift during 2026 ran both ways: Stradowe Meadows, Cherryvalley Park, Johnstown Demesne, Mason Cross and Oldtown Woods rose by €15,000–€25,000, while Chapel Park's Elm fell from €475,000–€495,000 to €450,000 and Abbey Park's 3-bed from "from €440,000" to €390,000 ([Irish Times, 24 Sept 2026](https://www.irishtimes.com/property/residential/2026/09/24/new-homes-your-guide-to-developments-on-the-market-across-ireland/); [Daft Chapel Park](https://www.daft.ie/new-home-for-sale/the-elm-chapel-park-kinnegad-co-westmeath-chapel-park-kinnegad-co-westmeath/6685713)). On transport, the Navan line is costed at €1bn by RTÉ and up to €3bn by the NTA, its Railway Order is dated 2027 by one Meath Chronicle piece and 2028 by RTÉ, and DART+ Coastal North's completion is given as 2029 or 2029–2035 depending on the source ([RTÉ, 24 May 2026](https://www.rte.ie/news/2026/0524/1574920-navan-railine/); [Irish Times, Dec 2024](https://www.irishtimes.com/transport/2024/12/04/proposed-navan-dublin-rail-line-likely-to-cost-up-to-3bn-transport-authority-estimates)). Almost no official 2026 journey time exists for Swords, Ashbourne, Ratoath, Blessington, Newbridge, Kildare town, Enfield or Kilcock, so their door-to-door verdicts above are inferred from position on the network rather than measured. The FHS mid-2026 review outcome was not published and the official ceilings page returned errors to one researcher; the figures used are those live on firsthomescheme.ie on 8 November 2026. Finally, the schools evidence is uneven: 2025 feeder-school rates were retrievable for Meath and Kildare but not for Louth, Fingal, Wicklow, Laois, Offaly or south Dublin, and no Pobal 2024/25 childcare figures exist yet for Dublin, Laois, Offaly, Westmeath, Carlow or Wexford, so the amenity ranking is firmest where it matters most (Balbriggan, Naas, Dunboyne, Navan) and weakest for Swords, Lusk and the midlands.

## Conclusion

The most useful finding is that the "one-hour commuter belt" and the "HTB-eligible 3-bed house" have almost stopped overlapping: developers have priced the entry house at exactly €500,000 everywhere the train is quick, and the only ways to buy a house under €470,000 within reach of the city are either to accept Balbriggan's 75 minutes or to win a council starter-home allocation, which is now the de facto first-time-buyer channel in Fingal, Naas and Drogheda rather than a niche. That makes the practical strategy a portfolio rather than a single bid: reserve a Folkstown Park (or, for a strict hour, Keepers Lock) unit on the open market as the certain option, and simultaneously lodge applications for Hampton Demesne's mid-2027 portal, Barracksfield West's December tranche and Fingal's rolling Balmoston and Golding Green releases, dropping the open-market reservation only if an allocation lands. Sign nothing before 7 October 2026 has passed (it has), apply for HTB in the calendar year you sign, and keep the FHS ceiling review of 1 January 2027 on your radar, because a €25,000 rise in Kildare or Meath would bring Dunboyne's €490,000 houses and Naas's open-market €475,000–€495,000 stock back inside the equity scheme.

The second implication is that the amenity evidence should be read as a warning about new-town risk rather than a tie-breaker between mature towns. Dunshaughlin's 80 unplaced children, Donabate's single college and cancelled crèches, and Clonburris's unbuilt school sites are the same story at different stages: housing approved faster than schools, childcare and GPs. Balbriggan, Naas and Drogheda carry their own problems, but their problems are visible, funded and dated, which for a family buying in 2027 is worth more than a marketing "family town" score.
