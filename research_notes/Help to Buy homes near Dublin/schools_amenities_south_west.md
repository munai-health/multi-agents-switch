# Schools and amenities in the southern and western Dublin commuter belt (Kildare, Wicklow, Laois, Offaly, Westmeath, Carlow, north Wexford, west/south Dublin) for a 2027 first-time buyer

Research date: 8 November 2026. All sources dated where the page gave a date. Scope limited to the 30 listed towns. Note: a large share of the evidence is Kildare-centric because that is where the 2025-26 school-place reporting and Department of Education announcements concentrate; coverage of the midland towns (Kinnegad, Edenderry, Mullingar, Tullamore, Portarlington, Portlaoise, Athy, Carlow) and the south Dublin SDZs is thinner and the Gaps sections say so.

## Key Question 1: Primary schools — number, enrolment pressure, new builds, multi-denominational options, estates far from schools

### Takeaway
Primary-place pressure in 2025-26 was most acute in Naas (eight schools full for junior infants 2026-27, ~30 children without a place in Feb 2026) and Portlaoise (council formally requesting two new primary schools, children bussed to rural schools), while the Department of Education forecasts falling primary-age populations across all Wicklow planning areas. The January 2026 NDP "first tranche" names concrete primary builds/extensions in Maynooth (new Gaelscoil Ruairí building, Presentation and Gaelscoil Uí Fhiaich extensions), Naas (Scoil Mhuire and Gaelscoil Nás na Ríogh extensions), Adamstown (new primary school building), Lucan (Lucan Boys modular extension) and Rathcoole (Scoil Chrónáin new building).

### Inferences
- Naas is the clearest "primary squeeze" town in Kildare for a 2027 buyer; the new 32-classroom Mercy Convent building plus St Corban's redevelopment should ease it, but the July 2026 teacher dispute shows capacity on paper does not guarantee staffed classes.
- Maynooth and Naas are the only towns in the list with multiple named primary extensions/new builds in the 2026-27 tranche, which suggests the Department sees them as the highest-growth primary catchments in Kildare.
- In Wicklow the Department's own forecast of falling primary-age populations means new estates in Kilcoole/Rathnew/Newtownmountkennedy are more likely to face "nearest school is full in a given year" problems than structural shortage.
- Portlaoise and Tullamore are the midland towns where primary shortage is officially acknowledged (council request for two schools; Department saying a new school is needed), relevant for buyers of larger new estates there.

### Gaps
- No single source gave a per-town count of primary schools for all 30 towns; counts above are only for Blessington, Edenderry, Portlaoise, Tallaght.
- No source identified specific new estates "far from the nearest school"; this would require mapping estate locations against school catchments, which the news coverage does not do.
- No 2025-26 primary-place shortage reporting was found for Leixlip, Celbridge, Clane, Sallins, Newbridge, Kildare town, Athy, Portarlington, Kinnegad, Mullingar, Edenderry, Carlow, Bray, Greystones, Wicklow town, Rathnew, Arklow, Gorey, Clondalkin or Tallaght (absence of reporting is not evidence of absence of pressure).
- Educate Together / Gaelscoil presence confirmed only where it surfaced incidentally (Maynooth, Naas, Blessington, Greystones, Tullamore, Lucan East, Gorey secondary); a full multi-denominational map per town was not obtained.

## Key Question 2: Secondary schools — number/types, capacity pressure, new post-primary builds by 2027, Leaving Cert progression indicators

### Takeaway
Post-primary capacity pressure is worst in north Kildare (reported waiting lists of 190 at Maynooth Community College and 200+ across Coláiste Chiaráin and Confey, Leixlip) and in Newbridge/Kildare town/Curragh, while the Department forecasts broadly flat post-primary demand in Wicklow to 2030 (slight rises in Kilcoole and Wicklow town, decline in Arklow). The most important confirmed deliveries for a 2027 buyer are: Greystones Community College's new 1,000-pupil Charlesland campus (completed Feb 2026, school operating from it in 2026-27); Coláiste Pobail Fóla Citywest Phase 2 to 1,000 capacity (due Jan 2028); and 2026-27 tender/construction starts for new 1,000-pupil buildings at Curragh Community College (Magee Barracks, Kildare town), Patrician Newbridge, St Mary's Naas, St Farnan's Prosperous, Coláiste Íosagáin Portarlington, plus extensions at Coláiste Chraobh Abhann Kilcoole, Blessington CC and Celbridge CS. On Irish Times 2025 feeder data, Kildare's top performers are Dominican College Newbridge (98%, fee-paying), St Mary's College Naas (93%), Maynooth Post Primary (90%), Holy Family Newbridge (87%), Ardscoil na Tríonóide Athy (86%) and Confey (84%).

### Inferences
Tier 1 — strongest overall for a family in 2027
1. Greystones — new 1,000-pupil Greystones CC open 2026; steady demand forecast; DART, beach, greenway consultation; new practices on the east coast; downside: north Wicklow childcare waiting lists, Wicklow burglary rate.
2. Naas — widest secondary choice (CBS, St Mary's 93%, Piper's Hill, Naas CC, Gael-Choláiste), new 1,000-pupil St Mary's funded, K Leisure pool (PoolMark), Naas General ED on the doorstep; downside: junior-infant squeeze 2026, ED overcrowding, Kildare childcare desert.
3. Maynooth — Maynooth PP at 90%, university town, new Gaelscoil Ruairí building and two primary extensions in 2026-27 tranche; downside: 190-student MCC waiting list, no pool until Leixlip study concludes.
4. Bray — largest Wicklow town, Irish Times "best places" (2021), seaside/parks, new GP practices, steady demand; downside: highest-county burglary rate, no 2025 feeder data retrieved.
5. Leixlip — Coláiste Chiaráin topped the 2024 national feeder list, Confey 84%; pool feasibility study under way with Intel €1m; downside: 200+ on Leixlip secondary waiting lists.
6. Celbridge — three post-primaries (St Wolstan's, Salesian 70%, Celbridge CS 66% + modular extension), Castletown parkland (Irish Times 2021 pick); downside: no new post-primary capacity beyond modular.
7. Newbridge — Dominican 98% (fee-paying), Holy Family 87%, Patrician 77% with new building funded; €15.2m county library under way; downside: primary and secondary shortage flagged May 2026, no pool, worst GP registration anecdote, €1,450/month crèche.
8. Mullingar — regional hospital with ED, Westmeath's top-tier GP coverage, council pool, IMC cinema, St Finian's build on site; downside: longest commute in Tier 1, no 2025 feeder data.
9. Tullamore — Midland Regional (zero trolleys in 2026 snapshots), Aura pool, IMC, Educate Together; downside: lottery-based secondary admissions, Department says a new primary school is needed.
10. Wicklow town / Rathnew — slight rise in demand forecast, new GP practices, cheaper than Bray-Greystones, rail; downside: 70-min rail, no new school build named.

11. Clane — Scoil Mhuire CS 71%, Clongowes (fee-paying 85%), new community library; St Farnan's Prosperous replacement approved Oct 2026 (no timeline).
12. Kilcoole — Coláiste Chraobh Abhann extension in 2026-27 tranche, primary expansion planned, Greystones CC nearby; demand rising slightly.
13. Sallins† — relies entirely on Naas schools/amenities (Naas pressures apply); no town-specific evidence found.
14. Blessington — seven primaries, Blessington CC extension/refurb funded, lakeside greenway; downside: named GP shortage area, no rail.
15. Athy — Ardscoil na Tríonóide 86%, K Leisure pool (PoolMark), ~65 min rail; Athy CC 55%.
16. Kildare town — Kildare Town CS 64%, Curragh CC replacement (1,000 pupils, Magee Barracks) at letter-of-intent stage; current Curragh CC "bursting" at 35% progression; shortage flagged May 2026; GP ratio 1:3,163 in south Kildare.
17. Gorey — three post-primaries incl. 1,546-pupil Gorey CS (places rising to 300) and ETSS; downside: Wexford burglaries +18%, no 2025-26 capacity or amenity news, long commute.
18. Arklow — demand declining (less capacity risk), St Mary's Park regeneration; downside: enrolment down 19% since 2015/16, CBS build stalled since 2020, no 2025 data.
19. Portlaoise — Odeon, leisure pool, rail; downside: council requesting two new primaries, children bussed out, 1,000-place secondary not started, special-ed "chronic shortage".
20. Carlow town — St Mary's Academy at design stage, Gaelcholáiste SEN extension granted; €60m pool undecided; St Luke's Kilkenny for ED.
21. Lucan / Adamstown — Adamstown CC oversubscribed (537 applications for 180 places), new Adamstown primary building and Lucan extensions in 2026-27 tranche; TD says community facilities lag.
22. Tallaght† — 25 primary/6 post-primary, Tallaght University Hospital; no 2025-26 shortage or amenity evidence found either way.
23. Citywest / Saggart — Coláiste Pobail Fóla Phase 2 to 1,000 due Jan 2028, new special school 2026; TD says facilities lag.
24. Clondalkin / Clonburris — six to eight school sites ring-fenced but none confirmed opening by 2027; relies on Kishoge CC and Lucan East ETNS.
25. Newtownmountkennedy† — 2018-promised second primary unconfirmed, GP practice "under strain"; depends on Greystones/Kilcoole secondaries.

### Gaps
- The ranking leans on Kildare feeder data and Wicklow demand forecasts; midland and south Dublin towns are placed with lower confidence because no feeder-school rates, waiting-list counts or amenity inventories were obtained for them.
- Sallins, Tallaght, Newtownmountkennedy and Kinnegad (†) are ranked on neighbouring-town or county evidence only.
