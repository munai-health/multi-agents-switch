# Irish first-time-buyer support schemes and mortgage rules for a new-build 3-bed in the Dublin commuter belt (completing 2027) — status as of 8 November 2026

Scope note: "Budget 2026" was delivered on 7 October 2025. The budget delivered on 6 October 2026 is **Budget 2027**, and it is the one that changed Help to Buy (effective 7 October 2026). All figures below are as of November 2026. Official sources (Revenue, Citizens Information, firsthomescheme.ie, localauthorityhomeloan.ie, Central Bank, council sites) are marked "official"; broker/comparison-site figures are marked "commercial".

## Key question 1: Help to Buy (HTB) after Budget 2027 — rate, cap, end date, FTB definition, 70% LTV, tax compliance, claim timing, 2026 contract / 2027 completion, self-build vs developer

### Takeaway
From 7 October 2026 HTB pays the lesser of €35,000, 10% of the purchase value, or the income tax + DIRT paid in the 4 years before the application year, on new builds/self-builds valued at €500,000 or less, for contracts signed (or self-build first drawdowns) between 7 October 2026 and 31 December 2029. The **contract-signing date, not the completion date, fixes which cap applies**, so a contract signed on/after 7 October 2026 that completes in 2027 gets the €35,000 cap; a contract signed before 7 October 2026 gets €30,000 even if completion is in 2027.

### Cited Findings
- Budget 2027 (announced 6 October 2026) raised the maximum HTB from €30,000 to €35,000; the €500,000 new-build price ceiling was deliberately left unchanged — [RTÉ, 6 Oct 2026](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2026/1006/1594234-housing-budget-2027/) (official broadcaster report of ministerial speech).
- "The maximum amount you can claim with the Help to Buy Scheme increased from €30,000 to €35,000 in Budget 2027. This increase applies from 7 October 2026. To get the increased amount, you must either: Sign the contract for your new house between 7 October 2026 and 31 December 2029 [or] Draw down on your self-build mortgage between 7 October 2026 and 31 December 2029." — [Citizens Information, HTB page edited 12 Oct 2026](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/help-to-buy-scheme/) (official).
- Revenue's three relief periods: 1 Jan 2017–22 Jul 2020 up to €20,000 (5%); 23 Jul 2020–6 Oct 2026 "enhanced relief" max €30,000 (10%); 7 Oct 2026–31 Dec 2029 "further enhanced relief" max €35,000 (10%). "The period is set by the date you sign a purchase contract, or the date of the first mortgage drawdown for a self-build." Relief is the lesser of the cap, 10% of purchase value/approved valuation, or income tax + DIRT paid in the 4 years before applying (USC and PRSI do not count). The cap is per qualifying property regardless of the number of buyers — [Revenue, How much can you claim](https://www.revenue.ie/en/property/help-to-buy-incentive/how-much-can-you-claim.aspx) (official).
- Already-approved applications: "If you have an approved HTB application that has not reached the claim stage, it will be automatically re-assessed, if you were getting the previous maximum relief of €30,000. Or you can cancel your application and re-apply" — [Citizens Information](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/help-to-buy-scheme/). MyHome adds that the increase "is not an automatic €5,000 top-up"; buyers already in conveyancing "may be able to cancel and reapply if their developer has not yet activated or verified the claim", and that 48,878 claims were approved from July 2020 to 22 July 2026 (~38,000 new homes, ~11,000 self-build) — [MyHome.ie](https://news.myhome.ie/myhome-new-living/help-to-buy-scheme-increased-to-e35000-38941) (commercial/news).
- Effective-date conflict: William Fry's Budget 2027 summary was reported as giving "7 October 2027"; this conflicts with Citizens Information and Revenue, which both say 7 October 2026 — [William Fry](https://www.williamfry.com/knowledge/budget-2027/) contradicted by [Citizens Information](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/help-to-buy-scheme/) and [Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/property/help-to-buy-incentive/how-much-can-you-claim.aspx). Treat 7 October 2026 as correct.
- First-time-buyer definition: you must be "a first-time purchaser of a property at the time of the claim"; you cannot have previously bought or built a house or apartment, alone or jointly, in or outside the State; all co-purchasers must be FTBs; inheritance/gifts of property may not disqualify you depending on circumstances — [Revenue, Who can claim](https://www.revenue.ie/en/property/help-to-buy-incentive/who-can-claim-htb.aspx) (official). Citizens Information is explicit that there is **no "fresh start" exception for HTB**: you will not qualify if you previously bought or built a home "even if you are now separated or divorced from that person and have given up your interest in the property", nor if you owned a home abroad — [Citizens Information](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/help-to-buy-scheme/).
- Mortgage/LTV: a mortgage from a qualifying lender of at least 70% of the purchase value (new build) or approved valuation (self-build) is required; a guarantor is allowed and need not be a FTB — [Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/property/help-to-buy-incentive/who-can-claim-htb.aspx); [Citizens Information](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/help-to-buy-scheme/).
- Shared-equity treatment in the 70% test: First Home Scheme equity is **not** counted (only the mortgage counts), so a €300,000 home with a €180,000 mortgage and €45,000 FHS equity fails (60% LTV). Starter Home Purchase Scheme (local authority) equity **is** counted for contracts signed after 10 October 2023, and the market value (not the discounted price) must be used — [Citizens Information](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/help-to-buy-scheme/); Revenue's manual: "for applicants availing of other housing schemes, e.g., the First Home Scheme, the shared equity funding received will not form part of the HTB loan-to-value" and "Funding provided by the State as part of a shared equity arrangement does not meet the criteria of a qualifying loan" — [Revenue TDM Part 15-01-46 (last updated Dec 2024)](https://www.revenue.ie/en/tax-professionals/tdm-wm/income-tax-capital-gains-tax-corporation-tax/part-15/15-01-46.pdf) (official).
- Tax compliance: "You must be fully tax compliant for 4 years immediately before your claim"; Form 12 (PAYE) or Form 11 (self-assessed) must be filed for each of those 4 years and outstanding tax paid; PAYE applicants registered for another tax need a Tax Clearance Certificate for those years; self-assessed applicants need all returns filed and liabilities paid — [Citizens Information](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/help-to-buy-scheme/); [Revenue TDM s.8](https://www.revenue.ie/en/tax-professionals/tdm-wm/income-tax-capital-gains-tax-corporation-tax/part-15/15-01-46.pdf).
- Property conditions: must be a new residential property never previously used/suitable for use as a dwelling; market value €500,000 or less (use market value, not the subsidised price, if buying under the Starter Home Purchase Scheme); developer must be a Revenue-approved qualifying contractor; self-builders need a solicitor registered as an "HTB approver"; you must live in it for 5 years from the date it is habitable — [Citizens Information](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/help-to-buy-scheme/); [Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/property/help-to-buy-incentive/who-can-claim-htb.aspx).
- Process: three online stages — application (via myAccount/ROS; approval gives an Application Number and a summary of the maximum amount, plus an Access Code via MyEnquiries for lender/contractor), claim (after contract signed, uploading the signed purchase contract and loan offer), verification (by the contractor, or solicitor for self-build). Refund is paid to the contractor (purchase) or to the applicant's account with the mortgage lender (self-build) — [Citizens Information](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/help-to-buy-scheme/); [Revenue TDM s.11](https://www.revenue.ie/en/tax-professionals/tdm-wm/income-tax-capital-gains-tax-corporation-tax/part-15/15-01-46.pdf).
- Application expiry (critical for 2026/2027 timing): an application expires on 31 December of the tax year in which it is made (or earlier if a condition is found unmet or tax clearance is rescinded). "A valid claim must be submitted before an application expires, otherwise an application will have to be resubmitted." Concession: if the application is made 1 Oct–31 Dec and the claim is made 1 Jan–31 Mar of the following year, the claim is deemed made in the prior year (Example 11.1). Example 11.3: Jane applied 1 Aug 2021 but did not sign a contract until Jan 2022; her application expired 31 Dec 2021 and she had to re-apply in 2022. The 4 "relevant tax years" are the 4 years immediately preceding the (deemed) application year — [Revenue TDM s.11.1.1](https://www.revenue.ie/en/tax-professionals/tdm-wm/income-tax-capital-gains-tax-corporation-tax/part-15/15-01-46.pdf).
- Completion/clawback: the contractor must repay HTB if the buyer has not completed the purchase within 2 years of the contractor receiving the payment (Revenue may be flexible if the home is nearly finished); self-build must be completed within 2 years of payment. Buyer clawback if you do not live there 5 years: 100% if you leave in year 1, 80% year 2, 60% year 3, 40% year 4, 20% year 5 — [Citizens Information](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/help-to-buy-scheme/); [Revenue TDM s.15](https://www.revenue.ie/en/tax-professionals/tdm-wm/income-tax-capital-gains-tax-corporation-tax/part-15/15-01-46.pdf).
- Self-build vs purchase differences: self-build uses the lender's approved valuation (including site cost) instead of purchase price; the trigger date is the first mortgage drawdown rather than contract signing; no qualifying contractor is needed but a Revenue-registered solicitor verifies; payment goes to the applicant's loan account rather than to a developer; site equity cannot substitute for the 70% mortgage test — [Citizens Information](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/help-to-buy-scheme/); [Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/property/help-to-buy-incentive/who-can-claim-htb.aspx).
- Industry criticism: Lisney CEO Aoife Brennan called the unchanged €500,000 ceiling "the biggest disappointment", saying GDA homes at €520,000–€550,000 are out of reach for HTB-reliant buyers and developers cannot viably sell at or below €500,000 — [MyHome.ie](https://news.myhome.ie/myhome-new-living/help-to-buy-scheme-increased-to-e35000-38941).

### Inferences
- For a buyer whose purchase completes in 2027: (a) if contracts are signed on/after 7 Oct 2026, the €35,000 cap applies irrespective of the 2027 completion; (b) if contracts were signed before 7 Oct 2026, the cap is €30,000 and the "cancel and re-apply" route does not change the contract date, so it would not help (the MyHome suggestion that in-conveyancing buyers can re-apply should be read as applying only where the contract is signed on/after 7 Oct 2026, or where the developer has not yet verified the claim; Revenue's wording ties the cap to the contract date).
- Timing of the application matters for both the tax look-back and expiry: an application made in 2026 is assessed on tax paid in 2022–2025 and dies on 31 Dec 2026 unless the claim is lodged (or, if applied Oct–Dec 2026, the claim is lodged by 31 Mar 2027). If the contract will not be signed until 2027, apply in 2027 (look-back 2023–2026). The HTB refund is paid to the developer at claim/verification stage, typically well before completion, and counts toward the contract deposit; completion in 2027 is fine provided it happens within 2 years of the payment.
- Because HTB maximum is 10% and the FTB deposit minimum is 10%, HTB can fund the entire 10% deposit only on prices up to €350,000; above that the buyer needs savings for the shortfall (see worked numbers in Key question 5). At €365k–€500k the €35,000 cap (not the 10% rule) binds, provided the buyer has paid ≥€35,000 income tax + DIRT over the 4 look-back years.

### Gaps
- Revenue's "how do you apply" subpage returned 404 and the Tax and Duty Manual on Revenue's site is still the December 2024 version (it describes the €30,000 "enhanced" relief, not the €35,000 "further enhanced" relief). The 7 Oct 2026 transition rule is documented only on Revenue's "How much can you claim" page and Citizens Information; Finance Bill 2026 text was not located.
- No official source was found explicitly addressing a contract signed between 7 Oct and 31 Dec 2026 where the HTB application was made before 7 Oct 2026 and the claim is made in 2027 — the general rules (contract date fixes the cap; application expiry/Oct–Dec concession) are what apply.

## Key question 2: First Home Scheme (FHS) — county price ceilings, equity share, maximum-mortgage requirement, income limits, review cycle

### Takeaway
FHS ceilings (identical for houses, apartments and self-build in every commuter-belt county) are €500,000 in the four Dublin authorities and Wicklow, €475,000 in Kildare and Meath, €425,000 in Louth, €400,000 in Laois, Westmeath and Wexford, and €375,000 in Carlow and Offaly. Equity is up to 30% of the price (20% if combined with HTB), minimum the higher of 2.5% or €10,000; there is no income limit, but you must borrow the maximum a participating lender (AIB/EBS/Haven, Bank of Ireland, PTSB) will give you under the 4x rule and put down 10%; ceilings are reviewed twice a year and the most recent change took effect 1 January 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Current ceilings on the official page (house / apartment / self-build): Dublin City, Dún Laoghaire-Rathdown, Fingal, South Dublin, Wicklow, Cork City €500,000; Kildare, Meath, Galway City €475,000; Cork County, Galway County, Limerick €450,000; Louth, Kerry, Mayo €425,000; Waterford €400,000 house / €450,000 apartment; Laois, Westmeath, Wexford, Clare, Donegal, Kilkenny, Leitrim, Roscommon, Sligo €400,000; Carlow, Offaly, Cavan, Longford, Monaghan, Tipperary €375,000. The page states only that ceilings "are regularly reviewed and may be subject to change" (no review date given) — [firsthomescheme.ie property price ceilings](https://www.firsthomescheme.ie/property-price-ceilings/) (official). The same table, with "Duplexes fall under the house price limits", appears on [Citizens Information FHS page](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/first-home-scheme/) (official).
- Last change: from 1 January 2026, following the "latest scheduled twice-yearly review", ceilings in 17 of 31 local authority areas rose by €25,000 (e.g., Cork City to €500,000, Galway County to €450,000, Cavan/Monaghan/Donegal to €375,000, Westmeath and Kerry included); reviews "continue to be carried out on a biannual basis" — [MyHome.ie](https://news.myhome.ie/myhome-living/first-home-scheme-extended-in-17-areas-nationwide-37053); [Anglo-Celt, 9 Jan 2026](https://www.anglocelt.ie/2026/01/09/new-e375k-price-ceiling-in-cavan-monaghan-under-first-home-scheme/); [Westmeath Examiner](https://www.westmeathexaminer.ie/?p=120739). Kildare was not increased in that round: "following a detailed review, it was decided not to increase the property price ceiling for Kildare at this time" — [KFM](https://www.kfmradio.com/news/localnews/no-first-home-scheme-increase-for-kildare-as-17-areas-see-e25k-rise/). Kildare's €475,000 dates from an earlier (1 July) review — [KFM](https://www.kfmradio.com/news/localnews/ceiling-to-qualify-for-first-home-scheme-in-kildare-rises-to-e475000).
- Equity: "Up to 30% of the purchase price or build cost, or 20% if you use Help to Buy (HTB)"; minimum 2.5% of price or €10,000, whichever is higher — [firsthomescheme.ie FAQs](https://www.firsthomescheme.ie/faqs/) (official); [Citizens Information](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/first-home-scheme/).
- Mortgage requirement: "You must borrow the maximum amount available to you from one of these lenders. Under Central Bank rules, the limit for mortgage borrowing for first-time buyers is 4 times your gross annual income"; borrowers getting a Macro Prudential Exception (above-limit loan) do not qualify; deposit at least 10% of purchase price and "If you are availing of the Help to Buy Scheme, this can contribute towards the deposit" — [Citizens Information](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/first-home-scheme/); [FHS FAQs](https://www.firsthomescheme.ie/faqs/).
- Income: "There is no upper or lower household income limit to be eligible for the FHS" — [FHS FAQs](https://www.firsthomescheme.ie/faqs/).
- Participating lenders "at the time of writing": Allied Irish Banks plc (AIB, Haven Mortgages, EBS), Bank of Ireland Group plc, PTSB — [FHS FAQs](https://www.firsthomescheme.ie/faqs/).
- Eligibility: first-time buyers (never bought/built, no interest in property in Ireland or abroad; owning abroad or having inherited a home disqualifies) or "fresh start" applicants (divorced/separated/relationship ended with no financial interest in the former home, or post-insolvency/bankruptcy); all co-buyers must qualify; over 18; property must be a new build in a private development (or self-build, or the Tenant Home Purchase variant) and your only residence — [Citizens Information](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/first-home-scheme/).
- Service charge on the equity (fixed for life of facility): 0% years 1–5; 1.75% years 6–15; 2.15% years 16–29; 2.85% year 30+; payable annually or monthly, can be reduced/paused/deferred — [FHS FAQs](https://www.firsthomescheme.ie/faqs/); [Citizens Information](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/first-home-scheme/). (Note: some commercial guides quote bands of 6–12 / 13–29 years; the official pages say 6–15 / 16–29.)
- Redemption: equity is a % of current market value (e.g., 10% on a €300,000 home becomes €35,000 when the home is worth €350,000); partial redemptions minimum 5% of original equity, max two per 12 months; full redemption required on sale, renting out, switching to a non-participating lender, or death; FHS-approved valuation (buyer pays) valid 12 months; value added by material improvements is excluded — [Citizens Information](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/first-home-scheme/); [FHS FAQs](https://www.firsthomescheme.ie/faqs/).
- Combination rules: FHS "can't be used with the Affordable Home Purchase Scheme or the Local Authority Home Loan"; it can be used with HTB (new build and self-build only) and Croí Cónaithe (Cities) — [FHS FAQs](https://www.firsthomescheme.ie/faqs/); LAHL side confirms "You cannot get a Local Authority Home Loan to purchase a home under the First Home Scheme" — [Citizens Information LAHL](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/local-authority-mortgages/).
- Process: eligibility calculator → mortgage approval in principle from a participating lender → online FHS application → FHS eligibility certificate (given to lender) → mortgage letter of offer uploaded → FHS customer contract signed (witnessed by solicitor) → funds sent to solicitor to complete — [Citizens Information](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/first-home-scheme/).
- Budget 2027 made no change to FHS (not mentioned in RTÉ's or Citizens Information's Budget coverage) — [RTÉ](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2026/1006/1594234-housing-budget-2027/); [Citizens Information Budget 2027](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/whats-new/how-does-budget-2027-affect-you/).

### Inferences
- The ceilings now on firsthomescheme.ie (Nov 2026) match the January 2026 figures for all commuter-belt counties, so either the mid-2026 review left these counties unchanged or no mid-2026 increase was announced; the next scheduled review would be 1 January 2027, which falls before a 2027 completion. A ceiling rise in Kildare/Meath (currently €475,000) or Louth (€425,000) in January 2027 is possible but unannounced.
- For a €500,000 purchase, FHS is available only in the four Dublin authorities and Wicklow; €450,000 works everywhere except Louth, Laois, Westmeath, Wexford, Carlow, Offaly; €400,000 works everywhere except Carlow and Offaly; €365,000 works in every listed county. Citizens Information says the home "must cost less than the price limit" while the FHS page uses "ceilings" — whether a price exactly at the ceiling qualifies should be confirmed with FHS.
- Because the FHS requires the maximum 4x mortgage and the 20% cap applies with HTB, the FHS equity in a stacked HTB+FHS purchase is simply price − 10% deposit − (4 × income), capped at 20%; the buyer cannot choose a smaller mortgage to enlarge the equity.

### Gaps
- No source states the outcome of the mid-2026 FHS review or a confirmed date for the next one; the official page gives no review dates.
- The FHS "how it works" page redirects to the application portal; the scheme's funding end-date / wind-down date was not found on official pages (the scheme is Government- and lender-funded; no termination date is published).

## Key question 3: Local Authority Home Loan (LAHL) — 2026 income limits, max loan, house price limits, interest rate

### Takeaway
Since 1 April 2026 the LAHL income limits are €80,000 (single) and €85,000 (joint), the loan is up to 90% of market value, and the house price limits are €415,000 in Dublin/Kildare/Wicklow, €375,000 in Meath, €345,000 in Louth/Westmeath/Wexford, and €310,000 in Laois/Offaly/Carlow; rates are 4.00% fixed (≤25 years, APR 4.07%) and 4.05% fixed (26–30 years, APR 4.13%), fixed for the full term, with compulsory group mortgage protection insurance.

### Cited Findings
- "The Minister for Housing, Local Government and Heritage implemented increases to the maximum eligible property prices and the income thresholds for applicants for this scheme from April 1st 2026"; loan "provides up to 90% of the market value of the property"; available to FTBs and fresh-start applicants for new, second-hand or self-build; expedited process for tenants with a Notice of Termination — [localauthorityhomeloan.ie](https://localauthorityhomeloan.ie/) (official).
- Income limits: single applicants €80,000 from 1 April 2026 (previously €70,000); joint applicants unchanged at €85,000 — [Citizens Information LAHL](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/local-authority-mortgages/) (official); [Minister Canney statement](https://www.seancanney.com/minister-sean-canney-welcomes-updated-limits-for-local-authority-home-loan-and-purchase-renovation-loan/); [RTÉ, 18 Feb 2026](https://www.rte.ie/news/2026/0218/1559081-ireland-politics/).
- Maximum property values from 1 April 2026 (previous in brackets): Dublin (all four), Kildare, Wicklow €415,000 (€360,000); Cork, Galway City, Meath €375,000 (€330,000); Galway County, Louth €345,000 (€330,000); Clare, Kilkenny, Limerick, Waterford, Westmeath, Wexford €345,000 (€300,000); Carlow, Cavan, Donegal, Kerry, Laois, Leitrim, Longford, Mayo, Monaghan, Offaly, Roscommon, Sligo, Tipperary €310,000 (€275,000) — [Citizens Information LAHL](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/local-authority-mortgages/); [Minister Canney](https://www.seancanney.com/minister-sean-canney-welcomes-updated-limits-for-local-authority-home-loan-and-purchase-renovation-loan/).
- Rates: "4.00% fixed interest rate for loans up to 25 years (APR 4.07%); 4.05% fixed interest rate for loans over 25 years and up to 30 years (APR 4.13%)"; rates "are set on the date you drawdown your loan"; must join the local authority collective Mortgage Protection Insurance scheme (paid monthly on top of repayments) — [Citizens Information LAHL](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/local-authority-mortgages/).
- Affordability: repayments "must be less than one-third of your household income" — [Citizens Information LAHL](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/local-authority-mortgages/).
- Eligibility: FTB or fresh start; aged 18–70; evidence of insufficient offers of finance from two regulated lenders; satisfactory credit record; 10% deposit of the lower of market price or purchase price (HTB can be used toward it); right to live in the State; owner-occupation; primary applicant 2 years' continuous employment (no gap >4 weeks), secondary applicant 1 year (certain long-term welfare payments accepted); decision in ~6–8 weeks; loan offer valid 6 months — [Citizens Information LAHL](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/local-authority-mortgages/).
- Those already approved in principle before 1 April 2026 can request the higher amount in writing without re-applying — [Citizens Information LAHL](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/local-authority-mortgages/).
- Combination: LAHL can be used with the Starter Home (affordable purchase) Scheme and Tenant Purchase (purchase price, not market price, is used for the eligibility test), and with HTB; it cannot be used with the First Home Scheme — [Citizens Information LAHL](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/local-authority-mortgages/).
- Commercial guides conflict on the rate (one lists "fixed rates from 3.35%"); the official/Citizens Information figures above (4.00%/4.05%) should be preferred — [search summary citing irishpropertyguide.ie and thegranthub.ie](https://thegranthub.ie/grants-ireland/local-authority-home-loan/) (commercial, unverified).

### Inferences
- For the four price points: a €365,000 house is within the LAHL cap in Dublin, Kildare, Wicklow and Meath but not Louth/Westmeath/Wexford (€345,000) or Laois/Offaly/Carlow (€310,000); €400,000 is only within the cap in Dublin/Kildare/Wicklow (€415,000); €450,000 and €500,000 exceed every county's cap, so LAHL is unavailable (except on a Starter Home purchase where the discounted purchase price, not the market value, is tested).
- The LAHL fixed 4.00–4.05% is now above the best commercial FTB fixed rates (≈3.0–3.5%, see Key question 5), so its value is for borrowers refused sufficient finance by two banks, not as a cheaper rate.
- LAHL 90% of €365,000 = €328,500; at 4.05% over 30 years that is ≈€1,568/month, which under the one-third rule implies household income of at least ≈€56,500 gross (plus MPI) — and the joint income cap of €85,000 remains binding.

### Gaps
- The April 2026 Credit Policy PDF on localauthorityhomeloan.ie returned HTTP 503 on two attempts, so the official loan-to-income multiple (if any) and the MPI premium rate could not be verified from the primary document.

## Key question 4: Local Authority Affordable Purchase Scheme (renamed Starter Home Purchase Scheme in 2026) — commuter-belt launches of 3-bed houses in 2026/2027, prices, eligibility

### Takeaway
The scheme was renamed the Starter Home Purchase Scheme in 2026 (advertised on NewStarterHomes.ie and council sites). In 2026 commuter-belt councils launched 3-bed houses at roughly €357,000–€395,000 after equity (Kildare: Barracksfield West, Naas, 3-beds from €365,216; Wicklow: Market Close, Dunlavin, 3-beds from €357,614; Fingal: Priory Fields, Skerries, 3-bed duplexes from €395,424; Louth: Ushers Mill, Drogheda, 3-beds, next phase "coming soon"; Meath: Ratoath, Dunboyne, Ballivor, Oldcastle, Rathmolyon schemes; South Dublin: Keepers Lock, Clonburris, with Killinarden and Kishoge Gardens expected around end-2026). Eligibility is a purchasing-power test (4 × gross income < 85.5% of market value), 10% deposit, FTB/fresh start, and 5–40% council equity repayable after 40 years.

## Key question 5: Central Bank rules, late-2026 mortgage rates, how the schemes stack, and worked numbers at €365k / €400k / €450k / €500k

### Takeaway
FTBs can borrow up to 4x gross income at up to 90% LTV (lenders may exceed the limits for 15% of FTB lending), rules unchanged since 1 January 2023 apart from an April 2026 bridging-loan tweak. Irish new-mortgage rates averaged 3.49% (June 2026) to about 3.5% (August 2026) with the best FTB fixed rates around 3.0–3.5% after two ECB hikes in 2026 (deposit rate 2.50% since 16 September 2026, held on 29 October). With HTB (€35,000) plus the maximum 20% FHS equity, the minimum household income needed is 17.5% of the price: ≈€63,900 at €365k, €70,000 at €400k, €78,750 at €450k and €87,500 at €500k; without FHS it is 22.5% of the price (€82,125 / €90,000 / €101,250 / €112,500).

### Cited Findings
- Central Bank limits: LTI 4x gross income for FTBs, 3.5x for second and subsequent buyers; LTV 90% (10% deposit) for FTBs and SSBs, 70% for buy-to-let; allowances of 15% of FTB lending, 15% of SSB lending and 10% of BTL lending may exceed the limits; "current limits took effect on 1 January 2023" — [Central Bank explainer](https://www.centralbank.ie/consumer-hub/explainers/what-are-the-mortgage-measures) (official). Allowances are applied at borrower-type level and unused allowance can be carried into H1 of the following year for fully approved loans — [Central Bank FAQ](https://www.centralbank.ie/consumer-hub/mortgage-measures/mortgage-measures---faq) (official).
- The 2022 framework review (effective 2023) raised the FTB LTI from 3.5x to 4x, kept 90% LTV, and widened the FTB definition so that borrowers who are divorced/separated or have been through insolvency and no longer have an interest in the previous property count as FTBs — [KPMG summary of the Central Bank review](https://home.kpmg/ie/en/home/insights/2022/12/mortgage-measures-framework-review.html); [Central Bank framework document](https://www.centralbank.ie/docs/default-source/financial-system/financial-stability/macroprudential-policy/mortgage-measures/framework-macroprudential-mortgage-measures.pdf).
- Only 2026 change: on 8 April 2026 the Central Bank exempted certain principal-home bridging loans from the LTI limit, "retaining the Loan-to-Value (LTV) limit and all other mortgage measures unchanged" — [Central Bank press release, 8 Apr 2026](https://www.centralbank.ie/news/article/press-release-targeted-amendment-to-mortgage-measures-home-bridging-loans-8-april-2026) (official). A Longford Leader article claims the FTB LTI was raised to 5x; this is contradicted by the Central Bank pages and by [TheJournal, April 2026](https://www.thejournal.ie/central-bank-mortgage-rules-bridging-loan-downsize-7006576-Apr2026) which still cites 4x — treat the 5x claim as erroneous — [Longford Leader](https://www.longfordleader.ie/news/property/942510/irish-home-buyers-to-benefit-from-new-changes-to-mortgage-lending-limits.html).
- The FHS requires the maximum 4x loan and excludes borrowers using a Central Bank allowance (Macro Prudential Exception) — [Citizens Information FHS](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/first-home-scheme/).
- Average rates (Central Bank data reported in press): June 2026 weighted average on new mortgage agreements 3.49% (euro area 3.51% — Ireland below the euro-area average for the first time since February 2023); fixed-rate loans averaged 3.46% and were 93% of volume; variable averaged 3.96% — [Irish Times, 12 Aug 2026](https://www.irishtimes.com/your-money/2026/08/12/mortgage-interest-rates-up-slightly-but-dip-below-euro-zone-average/). A later report put the August 2026 average at 3.53% (fixed 3.49%, 94% of volume) — [search summary; primary source is the Central Bank retail interest rates page](https://www.centralbank.ie/statistics/data-and-analysis/credit-and-banking-statistics/retail-interest-rates) (August figure not directly verified on the Central Bank page).
- ECB: the deposit facility rate has been 2.50% since 16 September 2026 (after hikes in June and September 2026); on 29 October 2026 the Governing Council held rates at 2.50% (deposit), 2.65% (main refinancing) and 2.90% (marginal lending) — [ECB monetary policy decision, 29 Oct 2026](https://ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/ecb.mp261029~9a493adf17.en.html) (official). Brokers quoted in August expected lenders to use a September ECB rise as a reason to raise mortgage rates; AIB, BoI and PTSB (≈90% of new lending) had not raised rates by then, most lenders did not pass on the June rise, and some niche lenders had already increased — [Irish Times, 12 Aug 2026](https://www.irishtimes.com/your-money/2026/08/12/mortgage-interest-rates-up-slightly-but-dip-below-euro-zone-average/).
- Headline lender rates (commercial guide updated 13 September 2026): lowest green (BER B3+) fixed rates AIB from 3.05% (standard 3.25%), Bank of Ireland from 3.20% (3.40%), Haven from 3.00% (3.15%), EBS from 3.10% (3.30%), PTSB from 3.25% (3.45%); Avant Money 3.35% at 81–90% LTV; headline lows are typically at lower LTV tiers — [mortgagebible.ie](https://mortgagebible.ie/mortgage-rates-ireland-2026/) (commercial). Other broker guides (spring–summer 2026) put most FTBs at 90% LTV in a 3.15–3.8% range depending on LTV, BER and lender — [mortgageline.ie](https://mortgageline.ie/best-mortgages-first-time-buyers-ireland/); [mmadvisors.ie](https://mmadvisors.ie/irish-mortgage-market-2026-rates-rules-and-whats-changed/) (commercial).
- Stacking rules used in the worked numbers: HTB = lesser of €35,000 / 10% / tax paid ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/property/help-to-buy-incentive/how-much-can-you-claim.aspx)); HTB can fund the FHS 10% deposit ([Citizens Information FHS](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/first-home-scheme/)); FHS equity max 20% with HTB, 30% without ([FHS FAQs](https://www.firsthomescheme.ie/faqs/)); mortgage must be the maximum 4x income ([Citizens Information FHS](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/first-home-scheme/)); HTB's 70% LTV test counts the mortgage only ([Citizens Information HTB](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/owning-a-home/help-with-buying-a-home/help-to-buy-scheme/)); FHS cannot be combined with LAHL or Starter Home purchase ([FHS FAQs](https://www.firsthomescheme.ie/faqs/)).

### Inferences
How the schemes stack (new-build developer purchase, FTB, no allowance):
- Route A (HTB + bank mortgage, no equity): deposit 10% (HTB up to €35,000 + savings), mortgage 90% ≤ 4x income.
- Route B (HTB + FHS + bank mortgage): deposit 10% (HTB + savings), mortgage = 4x income (must be the maximum), FHS equity = price − deposit − mortgage, capped at 20% of price → mortgage must be ≥70% of price, which also satisfies HTB's 70% LTV test exactly. Income needed ≥ 17.5% of price.
- Route C (FHS without HTB): equity up to 30%, mortgage ≥60% of price, income ≥ 15% of price, but the full 10% deposit must come from savings and HTB is forfeited (HTB would fail its 70% test).
- Route D (LAHL + HTB): only where the price is within the county LAHL cap and household income ≤ €85,000 joint / €80,000 single; no FHS.
- Route E (Starter Home purchase + HTB + bank/LAHL): council equity 5–40%; no FHS.

Worked numbers (assumes buyer has paid ≥€35,000 income tax + DIRT over the 4 look-back years, so the €35,000 cap binds at all four prices; monthly repayments are the researcher's own annuity calculations over 30 years, not lender quotes):

| €365,000 | €35,000 | €36,500 (€1,500) | €328,500 / €82,125 / ≈€1,475 | €73,000 / €255,500 / €63,875 / ≈€1,147 | €109,500 / €219,000 / €54,750 |

- Monthly cost sensitivity (per €1,000 borrowed, 30-year term): ≈€4.22 at 3.0%, ≈€4.49 at 3.5%, ≈€4.77 at 4.0%. E.g., the €280,000 Route-B mortgage at €400k costs ≈€1,181 (3.0%), €1,257 (3.5%), €1,337 (4.0%); the LAHL rate of 4.05% on €328,500 (90% of €365k) ≈ €1,568.
- Income between the Route-B minimum and the Route-A figure yields a smaller FHS stake: e.g., at €400,000 a household on €80,000 borrows €320,000 (80%), deposit €40,000, FHS equity €40,000 (10%). FHS equity shrinks €4 for every €1 of extra income because the mortgage must be maximised.
- Scheme ceilings by price point: €365k — HTB yes; FHS yes in all listed counties; LAHL yes in Dublin/Kildare/Wicklow/Meath only. €400k — HTB yes; FHS yes except Carlow/Offaly (€375k); LAHL Dublin/Kildare/Wicklow only. €450k — HTB yes; FHS yes in Dublin, Wicklow, Kildare, Meath (not Louth €425k, not Laois/Westmeath/Wexford €400k, not Carlow/Offaly); LAHL no. €500k — HTB yes (at the cap; €500,001 would lose all HTB); FHS only Dublin and Wicklow; LAHL no.
- Rate outlook for a 2027 drawdown: with the ECB on hold at 2.50% after two 2026 hikes and the pillar banks not yet having passed them on by August, a prudent planning rate for a 90%-LTV FTB fixed in 2027 is 3.3–3.8% (commercial-guide range), with the Central Bank average around 3.5%.

### Gaps
- No lender rate table dated November 2026 was retrievable (bonkers.ie comparison page showed no rates to the fetch tool); the September 2026 broker figures are the most recent available and may have moved after the September ECB hike.
- The August 2026 Central Bank average (3.53%) comes from a secondary summary and was not verified against the Central Bank's own release; the September 2026 release was not found.
- The worked numbers ignore stamp duty, legal fees, the FHS/HTB interaction with individual tax paid (a buyer who paid less than €35,000 tax over four years gets less HTB and needs more savings), and lender-specific affordability/stress tests, which can cap borrowing below 4x.

## Key question 6: Announced or proposed changes for 2027

### Takeaway
The only enacted 2027-relevant change to these schemes is Budget 2027's HTB rise to €35,000 (effective 7 October 2026, running to 31 December 2029). No changes to FHS, LAHL, the Starter Home scheme or Central Bank LTI/LTV limits were announced for 2027; the FHS twice-yearly ceiling review (next due around 1 January 2027) is the main scheduled item to watch.
