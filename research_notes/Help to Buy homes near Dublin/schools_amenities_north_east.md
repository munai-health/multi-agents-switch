# Schools and amenities in the northern and eastern Dublin commuter belt (Louth, Meath, Fingal, Kilcock) for a first-time buyer of a new-build 3-bed in 2027

Scope: Drogheda, Dundalk, Bettystown/Laytown, Duleek, Navan, Ashbourne, Ratoath, Dunshaughlin, Dunboyne, Trim, Kells, Enfield, Kilcock, Balbriggan, Skerries, Rush, Lusk, Donabate, Swords. Research conducted 8 November 2026 from web sources; dates of each source are noted inline. Coverage is uneven: Meath (Meath Chronicle / Meath Live) and Balbriggan (Our Balbriggan / Dublin People) are well reported; Louth and the smaller Fingal towns (Rush, Lusk, Donabate, Skerries) and Kilcock/Enfield are thinly reported and are flagged as gaps.

## Key Question 1: Primary schools — number, enrolment pressure, new builds, multi-denominational options, estates far from schools

### Takeaway
The single clearest primary-place crisis in the whole study area in 2025-26 is Dunshaughlin (c.80 children on waiting lists for Sept 2026, all three town schools oversubscribed, permanent buildings for Dunshaughlin CNS still not started five years after planning). Elsewhere, pressure shows up as capital projects (Navan's Ard Rí CNS campus, Duleek Girls NS extension, Marymount NS Drogheda) and as closed enrolment at individual Drogheda schools, but no other town has a reported 2026 shortfall on Dunshaughlin's scale. Educate Together primaries exist in Navan, Trim, Ashbourne, Drogheda (x2), Swords, Skerries and Balbriggan; Gaelscoileanna are under pressure in Dunshaughlin.

### Inferences
- Dunshaughlin is the one town where a 2027 buyer with a child born after May should assume a real risk of no local primary place in the first year; Cnoc Tiarnach and The Willows are named as affected estates. Ratoath/Dunboyne/Culmullen overflow absorbs some demand but those schools also have waitlists.
- Drogheda's "common enrolment" group closing all classes by June 2026 suggests central Drogheda primaries are at capacity, but the Marymount and Ballymakenny/Aston Village (ET) expansions on the north and south sides give some headroom; the Louth six-project list likely adds more but could not be verified.
- Fingal's new-build towns (Lusk, Donabate, Balbriggan) got primary capacity in the 2016-2024 wave (Lusk's two new schools, Balbriggan's special school); the risk there is secondary and crèche rather than primary.
- Ashbourne's shortage of the late 2010s has been addressed by Ashbourne CNS (opened 2024) and Ashbourne ETNS; no 2025-26 shortfall was reported.

### Gaps
- No reliable per-town count of primary schools from a primary source (Department of Education school lists were not fetched); the commercial counts above are unverified.
- No 2025-26 primary-place shortage reporting was found for Navan, Trim, Kells, Ratoath, Dunboyne (beyond parent anecdote), Enfield, Kilcock, Bettystown/Laytown, Dundalk, Swords, Rush, Lusk, Donabate or Skerries — absence of reporting is not proof of no pressure.
- The Louth six-project article could not be read (403); the named Louth projects are unknown.
- No source identified specific new-build estates "far from the nearest school" other than the Dunshaughlin estates; Donabate's 2024 approvals are the closest analogue.
- Gaelscoil provision: only Gaelscoil na Ríthe (Dunshaughlin) is sourced here.

## Key Question 3: Childcare — crèche availability and cost

### Takeaway
Pre-subsidy full-day fees are highest in Fingal (median c.€231/week, 3rd highest of 30 divisions, 2023/24), around €200/week in Meath (9th), and c.€186/week in Louth. Waiting lists are long everywhere: 1,400+ children in Louth, 1,038 in Fingal (2023/24 Pobal) and 13,500+ under-3s across Dublin (2025). Fingal has an unusually thin community/not-for-profit sector (16 public vs 274 private services; Balbriggan has one community crèche) and councillors report developer-promised crèches in Swords, Lusk and Donabate not materialising.
