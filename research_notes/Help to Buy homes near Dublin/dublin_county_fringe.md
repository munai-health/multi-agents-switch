# New-build 3-bed houses at or under €500,000 in outer County Dublin (Help to Buy eligible), 2026–2027

Research date: 8 November 2026. All prices are asking/"from" prices as published on the date noted beside each; HTB cap is €500,000 (new builds only). Where a figure comes from a listing whose date could not be verified it is flagged "date unverified / possibly stale".

Note on the research record: the Irish Times publishes two comprehensive new-homes guides a year (Feb and Sept). Both 2026 guides were retrieved in full and are the backbone of the inventory below; they are cross-checked against developer sites, Daft.ie, Dublish.ie and council affordable-housing pages.

## Key Question 1: Which schemes in County Dublin still have 3-bed HOUSES at €500,000 or under in 2026? (Development-by-development inventory for the outer fringe)

### Takeaway
Open-market 3-bed **houses** at or below €500,000 are now rare in County Dublin and concentrated in the far north of Fingal (Balbriggan, Donabate, Lusk, Swords) and in the Clonburris/Seven Mills SDZ in Dublin 22; almost every scheme that still prices a 3-bed under the cap prices it at exactly €500,000 or within €35,000 of it (Folkstown Park €465k–€485k; Balmoston €500k; Regles €500k; Hamlet Wood €500k; Keepers Lock €480k; Wicker Walk duplex €475k; Millers Glen €500k). Everywhere in South Dublin west (Adamstown, Lucan, Citywest, Newcastle, Hansfield), Dún Laoghaire-Rathdown (Cherrywood, Shankill, Kilternan) and Kinsealy, 3-bed houses now start at €520,000–€710,000 and sit outside HTB, leaving council Starter Home (affordable purchase) schemes as the only sub-€500k route (see KQ2).

## Key Question 5: Evidence that developers price 3-beds at or just under €500,000 to stay inside the HTB cap, and the effect of the Minister's October 2026 refusal to raise the cap

### Takeaway
There is strong statistical and anecdotal evidence of price bunching at €500,000 (new-build bunching ratio 2.33 nationally, 2.62 in Dublin, ~€11,700 average price compression per bunched sale), and the pattern is visible in the inventory above (Balmoston, Regles, Hamlet Wood, Corballis View, Mason Cross at launch all at exactly €500,000). The Minister kept the cap at €500,000 in Budget 2027 (raising the refund to €35,000) explicitly to "push builders" to keep prices under it; it is too early for sourced evidence that this is changing what gets built, though the 2026 guides show developers shifting sub-cap product toward duplexes and 2-beds.

### Cited Findings
- Budget 2027 (6–7 Oct 2026): HTB refund raised €5,000 to €35,000; Minister James Browne ruled out raising the €500,000 threshold, saying it "helps control the overall costs of new builds" and pushes builders to improve productivity; he does not believe HTB is "a key driver of house price increases"; 48,878 applications approved since 2020 — [Irish Times 7 Oct 2026](https://www.irishtimes.com/your-money/2026/10/07/minister-rules-out-raising-cap-on-help-to-buy-scheme-for-homes-worth-over-500k/); [RTÉ 6 Oct 2026](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2026/1006/1594234-housing-budget-2027/). Browne had lobbied in September for a €50,000 refund — [Irish Times 21 Sept 2026](https://www.irishtimes.com/politics/2026/09/21/minister-for-housing-in-fresh-bid-to-boost-help-to-buy-funding-in-budget-despite-pushback/). Finance Minister Simon Harris ruled out extending HTB to second-hand homes — [Irish Times 7 Oct 2026](https://www.irishtimes.com/your-money/2026/10/07/minister-rules-out-raising-cap-on-help-to-buy-scheme-for-homes-worth-over-500k/).
- Bunching evidence: using 768,074 PPR transactions 2010–2025, new builds show a bunching ratio of 2.33 (SE 0.11) just below €500,000 (≈1,806 transactions shifted, average compression ≈€11,700); second-hand homes show none (−0.20); Dublin 2.62 vs 2.10 outside Dublin; strongest post-2022 (2.55); "pricing a unit at €499,000 instead of €510,000 costs €11,000 in revenue but preserves HTB eligibility worth up to €25,000 to the buyer" — [APEP "The Developer's Ceiling", 2 April 2026](https://ape.socialcatalystlab.org/papers/apep_1297/v1/paper.pdf). (Note: an autonomous-research preprint, not peer-reviewed.)

### Inferences
- The combination of a fixed €500k cap, a €35k refund and First Home Scheme equity (20% with HTB) makes a €500,000 3-bed the "sweet spot" product; developers in Fingal (Glenveagh, Dwyer Nolan) are holding 3-bed entry prices at exactly €500,000 while selling larger/semi-detached types above it.
- Where land/build costs no longer allow a house at €500k (Adamstown, Citywest, Lucan, D18), the sub-cap offer has migrated to 3-bed duplexes and 2-beds, which is the most visible "what gets built" response so far.
- Any measurable supply response to the October 2026 decision will only appear in 2027 launches; nothing sourced yet attributes a design or tenure change to the Budget 2027 decision specifically.
